Computing in the World

Bias, accessibility, authorship, and energy — the visible consequences that computation leaves in the world.

Start Concept 17 Teacher guide

Concepts 17 – 20  ·  AP CSP IOC core

What this section covers

Code has consequences. This section uses the canvas as a lens to examine four of them: how algorithmic filters can fail certain users, how color choices affect who can read your work, who gets credit when an algorithm makes something, and how much energy computation actually uses. Each concept is taught through a visible demo, not a lecture.

  • Bias: a skin-tone filter applied to different inputs side by side
  • Accessibility: WCAG contrast ratios shown as colored rectangles
  • Authorship: a generated pattern vs. a hand-coded one — what differs?
  • Energy: frame budget, GPU load, and what "efficient code" means visually
  • Discussion prompts and facilitation notes for each concept

AP alignment

CSP IOC DAT CRD

This section directly covers AP CSP Big Idea 5 (Impact of Computing). Bias, privacy, accessibility, and digital citizenship questions appear on every AP CSP exam. The canvas demos give students concrete examples to reference in written responses.

Teacher framing

Facilitation stance: Your job is to hold the space, not supply the answer. Ask "what do you notice?" before "what does this mean?" Students who arrive at ethical conclusions through observation retain them differently than students who are told.

AP connection: Have students practice writing IOC evidence paragraphs using the canvas demos as their example systems.

Learner framing

Every choice you make when writing code — which colors, which algorithm, how much CPU you use — affects real people. Some of those effects are obvious. Many aren't.

This section doesn't tell you what to think. It shows you what's happening, and then asks you to think.

Discussion moves

  • "Who is this designed for?"
  • "Who is left out by this choice?"
  • "Could the code be changed? What would it take?"
  • "Is this a technical problem or a human one?"
