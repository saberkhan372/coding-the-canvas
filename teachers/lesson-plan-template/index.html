The repeatable rhythm

Give every lesson the same reliable shape.

Students learn faster when the class session has a predictable rhythm. The five-phase structure below works for any concept in the curriculum — adjust the timing for 30-minute and 50-minute periods.

  • Hook: visual question before syntax
  • Demo: teacher codes live, students watch
  • Practice: students run and trace the example
  • Remix: students change something with intention
  • Checkpoint: students explain one thing they made

The five phases in detail

phase 1Hook5 min
phase 2Live Demo10 min
phase 3Practice10 min
phase 4Remix15 min
phase 5Checkpoint10 min

Phase 1 — Hook 5 min

Show a visual question before any code. Project the canvas output of the day's concept and ask: "What do you think is making this happen?" or "How would you build this?" Let students guess. The wrong answers are fine — the hook is to create a question the demo will answer.

Teacher tip: Don't narrate the output — ask about it. Silence is okay. The hook works when students feel curious, not when they feel lectured.

Phase 2 — Live Demo 10 min

Code live with students watching (or walk through the concept page side by side). Narrate your thinking: "I'm using mouseX here because it updates every frame…" Start minimal — one shape, one rule — then build up. Make one deliberate mistake and fix it visibly. Students should see debugging as part of coding, not a sign of failure.

Teacher tip: Project the concept page alongside your code editor. The mini-canvas SVG gives students a visual reference while you type.

Phase 3 — Practice 10 min

Students open the concept page and run the example code. Their first goal is just to get it working. Ask them to read through each line and annotate with a comment: "// this draws a circle at the mouse position." Reading + annotating beats passive copying.

Teacher tip: Circulate and ask "what does this line do?" rather than answering questions directly. Redirection to the canvas output ("try changing that number and see what happens") is often more powerful than explanation.

Phase 4 — Remix 15 min · the core

Give a prompt: "Make it your own in one way. Change something with a reason." This is the heart of the lesson. Students who finish early get a harder prompt: "Add a second behavior" or "Make it respond to a keyboard key." The canvas is the immediate feedback loop — if the output changes, something happened.

Teacher tip: Write the remix prompt on the board before the lesson starts. Having it visible means you don't have to stop the class to issue it — students can move into remix naturally when they're ready.

Phase 5 — Checkpoint 10 min

Students take a canvas screenshot and write one sentence: "I changed [X] because [reason], and the result was [Y]." Collect it or add it to the portfolio. End with one volunteer sharing their remix. This closes the loop: demo → your version → explained to someone else.

Teacher tip: The checkpoint is not graded for correctness — it's graded for presence. "I tried X and it didn't work because Y" is a full-credit checkpoint response. What matters is that students engaged with the concept.

Adapted from the CC Fest Teacher Camp three-level assignment structure

Level 1

Foundation

Get something on the canvas. Run the example, annotate each line, change one value. The goal is contact with the idea — not mastery. Minimum viable: it runs and you can explain what one line does.

Level 2

Exploration

Push the idea further. Change two or more things with intention. Ask: "What happens if…?" The canvas is your scratch pad. Show at least one thing that surprised you — even a failed experiment counts.

Level 3

Classroom Adaptation

Redesign the concept for your students. Write one prompt, pick the right entry point, choose a language. Bring one concrete idea you would do Monday. Share with a partner — can they follow your plan?

30-minute period

Compress: combine hook + demo (8 min), skip written annotations during practice (5 min), shorten remix (12 min), skip whole-class share during checkpoint (5 min). The remix phase is protected — don't cut it.

Two-period lessons

Concepts 07 (Functions), 09 (Objects), and 21 (Recursion) benefit from two periods. Day 1 = phases 1–3. Day 2 = open with a short recap, then extended remix (20–25 min) followed by checkpoint + gallery walk.

Differentiation

Early finishers get extension prompts from the concept page's Teacher Notes. Students who struggle stay in Phase 3 (practice/annotate) — don't push them to remix until the example runs. Keep a "minimum viable remix" prompt (change one number) for students who need it.
