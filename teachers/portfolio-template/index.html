Purpose of the portfolio

Make learning visible over time.

A portfolio isn't a folder of finished products — it's a record of thinking. Each entry captures a moment of understanding: what the student made, what they changed, what they noticed, and what they want to try next.

  • One entry per major checkpoint (7 total for the full arc)
  • Canvas screenshot + code excerpt as the artifact pair
  • Written reflection in student's own words (2–4 sentences)
  • Optional: a question the student still has
  • Teacher reads for evidence of thinking, not perfection

Teacher instructions

Give students 10–15 minutes to complete an entry at the end of a checkpoint session. The screenshot takes 1 minute. The code excerpt takes 2. The writing is the work.

Accept all honest reflections. "I don't understand why this works" is a full-credit response — it shows metacognition.

Grading the portfolio

Grade on the rubric from the Assessment & Rubrics page: reproduce (1), modify (2), extend (3). The portfolio earns its level based on the explanation quality, not the visual complexity of the sketch.

A student who changed one value and clearly explains why earns a 2. A student with a dazzling sketch who can't explain their code earns a 1.

AP Create PT connection

For AP CSP, the §4 portfolio entry is the strongest Create PT seed: it already has a list, a procedure, and an algorithm. Students can expand it into a full CPT submission. See the Create PT support page.

For AP CS A, §4 and §7 entries generate authentic FRQ evidence: class design, array traversal, ArrayList methods.
