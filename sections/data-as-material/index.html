Concepts 11 – 16  ·  AP CSP data core

What this section covers

Every image is a grid of numbers. Every color is 24 bits. Every file on disk is a compressed sequence of patterns. This section makes those invisible facts visible — students inspect pixels, build binary images from scratch, and draw charts from CSV data. The canvas becomes a microscope for how computers store and manipulate information.

  • getPixels() / loadPixels() — reading image data
  • RGB as three 8-bit numbers (0–255 each)
  • Binary: 0s and 1s as black and white pixels
  • Run-length encoding demonstrated on canvas
  • Histograms and sampling from real datasets
  • Loading CSV/JSON and drawing from it

This section is the spine of AP CSP's Data unit (DAT-1 through DAT-2). Binary, compression, and metadata questions appear consistently on the AP exam. Loading and transforming datasets ties directly to Create Task expectations.

Teacher framing

Entry point: Start with pixel inspection on a 10×10 image students draw themselves. The moment they realize their drawing is just 300 numbers is the key "aha."

AP anchor: Concepts 12 and 13 (24-bit color, binary pixels) directly prepare students for DAT-1 multiple choice. Keep the canvas connection — it makes abstract bit-counting concrete.

Learner framing

Every photo on your phone is just numbers. Every emoji is a grid of colored dots. Every file you've ever saved is a long sequence of 0s and 1s that a program knows how to read.

In this section you'll write the programs that decode that data — and you'll see it appear on screen as you go.

Common misconceptions

  • Pixels() array stores RGBA, not just RGB (4 values each)
  • White is 255,255,255 — not 1,1,1
  • Compression doesn't always make files smaller
  • loadTable() is async — data isn't ready until preload()
