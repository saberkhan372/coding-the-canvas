Come in. You can learn here. You can make something.

October 2016, a rainy Saturday at NYU ITP. Forty people showed up. Dan Shiffman gave a keynote on tool neutrality. Teachers built classroom projects. Students made creative portfolios. No one left empty-handed — everyone left with something visible on a canvas.

That was the idea: code should produce something you can point to, explain, and keep making. CC Fest has been carrying that idea forward ever since — through in-person events in New York, Los Angeles, and San Francisco; through a virtual pivot in 2020 that made it global; through Teacher Training Camps that started in 2024. Ten years, and still 100% free.

  • Code as creative and cultural expression, not just a technical skill.
  • Hands-on coding with immediate, visible results.
  • No prerequisites. No judgment. Just curiosity.
  • Peer teaching, where students become mentors.

CC Fest Teacher Training Camp

Learn creative coding. Then ask how someone else could learn it too.

The Teacher Camp is a free, five-week virtual program — two hours a week — where educators code in p5.js, learn with guests from the creative coding community, and figure out how to bring that experience into their own classrooms. No prerequisites. Just curiosity and a willingness to make things.

Participants leave with working sketches, classroom-ready lesson ideas, and a community of educators who are also figuring it out. The camp has run every semester since Fall 2024 and is still free.

  • 5 sessions · 2 hours each · 100% free
  • Foundation, Exploration, and Classroom Adaptation tracks
  • Guest speakers from the creative coding community
  • Async recordings posted after every session

For students

Come in. Your first program can be something you actually made — visual, personal, yours. You do not have to know what you are doing yet. That is what the camp is for.

For teachers

Coding the Canvas turns the CC Fest workshop energy into scope, sequence, AP alignment, pacing guides, and assessment ideas you can actually use on Monday — or next semester.

For the community

CC Fest has always been free and public — built to be generous, remixable, and worth returning to. Made by Saber Khan with support from the Processing Foundation and the people who show up every semester.

AI, consent, and human-first learning

Use tools carefully. Keep people at the center.

AI can help draft recaps, debug examples, and prototype tools, but it should not replace live coding, participant reasoning, creative agency, consent, or community conversation.

Tools are optional. People come first.

Participants control what they share. Naming, credit, and permission matter.
