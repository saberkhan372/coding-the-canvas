Whose Authorship?

Generative art is made by a person and an algorithm. This page asks where choice, credit, and responsibility live.

Try it in the Playground Section overview

Concept 19 / 22

The big idea

A generative sketch contains human decisions: the rules, the randomness, the constraints, the dataset, and the act of curation. Students trace which parts of the output came from code and which came from the person who wrote or selected it.

  • Identify human choices in a generative sketch
  • Identify algorithmic choices
  • Distinguish randomness from authorship
  • Write an attribution statement
  • Discuss remix and credit

The canvas idea

Run the same rule with three seeds. Ask: what did the programmer choose, what did the computer choose, and what did the artist choose by selecting one output?

Patterns to notice

  • Seed
  • randomness
  • curation
  • attribution
  • remix

Teacher notes

This works well as a short writing prompt after a generative sketch project. Students can write an authorship note for their own work.

Common misconception: Random does not mean authorless. Someone chose the rules, ranges, colors, and stopping point.
